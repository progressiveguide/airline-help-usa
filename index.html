Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to the questions travelers ask us most.

Can I get a refund on a non-refundable ticket?

Yes — in specific circumstances. If the airline cancels or significantly changes your flight (3+ hour delay in departure time, origin/destination change, downgrade in cabin class), you are entitled to a full cash refund under U.S. DOT rules, regardless of fare type. If you initiated the cancellation, a Basic Economy ticket typically forfeits all value, while Main Cabin and higher fares often convert to travel credit. Check your carrier guide for exact policies.

How long does an airline refund take?

Most airlines issue refunds within 7 business days for credit cards and 20 business days for cash/check. If you haven't seen yours after that window, it's appropriate to call the airline directly. If you're past 30 days with no resolution, you can dispute the charge with your credit card issuer or file a complaint with the DOT.

What is the 24-hour cancellation rule?

Under U.S. DOT rules, airlines must allow passengers to cancel or hold reservations at the quoted price for 24 hours — provided the booking was made at least 7 days before departure. This applies to tickets purchased directly from the airline. Third-party bookings (OTAs) may have different policies.

What happens to travel credits if I don't use them?

It depends on the airline. Most credits issued as "flight credit" or "eCredit" expire within 12 months of the original booking or cancellation date — though many airlines extended validity during the pandemic. Always check the expiration date on your credit and apply it to a future booking before it lapses. Our carrier guides detail how to find and redeem your credit for each major airline.

What counts as a "significant schedule change" that qualifies for a refund?

The DOT defines a significant change as a departure or arrival time that differs by 3 hours or more (domestic) or 6 hours or more (international), a change to a different origin or destination airport, an increase in the number of connections, a downgrade in cabin class, or connections at different airports. If any of these apply, you can request a full refund, not just a credit.

Should I change or cancel my flight?

Changing is usually better if you still want to travel on a different date — especially for non-refundable tickets where a change preserves credit value. Cancelling makes sense if you want a refund and qualify for one (e.g., airline-initiated cancellation), or if you're unlikely to use the credit before expiration. Compare the change fee plus fare difference vs. the value of any credit before deciding.

Editorial Review Standards

We hold ourselves to a strict editorial process because traveler decisions made on bad information have real financial consequences. Here is exactly how our content is researched, written, and maintained.

Research & Sourcing

  • Every policy claim is sourced directly from the airline's official terms and conditions, help center, or DOT filings.
  • We cite the source page and date of access for every policy we describe.
  • We do not rely on community forums, social media, or unverified third-party sources for policy facts.

Writing & Review Process

  • Articles are drafted by a travel content specialist and reviewed by a second editor before publication.
  • Complex policy interpretations (e.g., DOT refund rules) are reviewed against official DOT guidance.
  • We avoid hedging language that dilutes actionable guidance. If the answer is "it depends," we explain exactly what it depends on.

Update Policy

  • Pages are reviewed after any confirmed airline policy change.
  • All pages carry a "last updated" date so readers can judge recency.
  • Outdated content is flagged, updated, or removed — not left to decay silently.
